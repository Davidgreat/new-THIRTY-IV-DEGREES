## One-line pitch
SchoolOS connects school records, staff workflows and verified family relationships in one modular operating platform for Nigerian schools.

## Problem
School teams often coordinate daily records across paper, spreadsheets and messages. Repeated entry, disconnected handovers and incomplete follow-ups are the product’s problem hypotheses. Pilot research should quantify their prevalence and cost.

## Product and first-use value
A school can start with pupil/class records, staff access, linked families, attendance and school-fee visibility. It can activate HR, library, store, transport, boarding and other supported responsibilities as needed. Guided setup and scoped permissions reduce the need to activate every role at once. Fictional demos demonstrate workflows; they are not paying customers.

## Business model
Schools pay a subscription based on pupil count, using progressive bands, a minimum term charge and monthly, four-month termly or annual billing. The reference annual-upfront saving is 10%, and a separate one-calendar-month trial reduces evaluation friction. At reference settings, 500 pupils cost ₦232,500 per term, ₦58,125 monthly or ₦627,750 annually upfront. Actual rates and offers remain editable and require a live quote.

Core subscriptions are separate from provider-funded usage. Assisted migration/training is optional and scoped through a quote/helpdesk workflow; no setup income is claimed. Tuition collected for a school is not platform subscription revenue.

## Distribution
The proposed path is founder-led school introductions, focused role demos and guided onboarding, supported by approved partners. The reference founding offer is capped at 20 qualifying paying schools and 500 pupils, with 20% off the first paid year. New referral commissions are reference 20% on eligible collected subscription money in the first 12 paid service months. Older referral terms are preserved. Current availability and eligibility are confirmed in-app.

## What funding should prove
Measure setup completion, weekly teacher usefulness, verified trial-to-paid conversion, renewal retention, price acceptance and cost to serve by school size. Proposed targets are hypotheses; the deck claims no measured school outcomes. A funding amount remains to be scoped against supplier-backed costs and milestones.

**0:20–0:45 — Problem.** Explain how scattered registers, spreadsheets and conversations can create repeated work and unclear follow-ups. Quantify this only after pilot evidence.

**0:45–1:10 — Product.** Show one school owner’s overview, guided setup and verified family links. Explain how schools activate only the roles and workflows they need.

**1:10–1:35 — Buyer and model.** Owners and administrators buy the subscription. The price scales with pupil count; core software and external usage costs are separate. Use the 500-pupil example if pricing detail is useful.

**1:35–2:00 — Adoption.** Start with founder-led introductions and a useful first workflow. Partners earn on eligible paid subscriptions, with a defined first-year allowance. Founding incentives are bounded.

**2:25–2:45 — Economics.** The model now derives revenue from school-size mix and subtracts founding savings, commissions, payment expenses and size-specific delivery costs. All cost/mix assumptions still need validation.

**2:45–3:00 — Ask.** Invite a milestone-based funding or accelerator discussion and suitable pilot-school introductions. Agree a costed ask rather than presenting the ₦30m appendix as a confirmed raise.
